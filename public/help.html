How gPhotos Desktop Works

A private, high-performance, on-device photo manager with zero cloud lock-in. Powered by local AI face recognition, intelligent duplicate clustering, and non-blocking background synchronization.

🔍

De-duplication & AI Burst Shot Selection

When taking photos with smartphones or cameras, people frequently capture identical duplicate shots or high-speed bursts to ensure everyone looks great. Our multi-stage AI deduplication engine detects, groups, scores, and cleans duplicate photos automatically.

Step 1 Temporal & Biometric Clustering

The engine scans your photos chronologically. It groups photos together into a single cluster if they satisfy any of the following:

  • Time Proximity: Photos taken within 20–60 seconds of each other.
  • Biometric Intersection: Exactly the same people detected across the candidate frames.
  • Identical Face Count: Equal number of subjects captured within 10 seconds.
  • Sequential Burst Filenames: Numbered sequence (e.g. IMG_0214.jpg, IMG_0215.jpg).

This is the Similar mode (the default). A second mode, Exact, switches to a much narrower match instead — only an identical file size and identical pixel dimensions, with no time window at all. Switch between them with the two buttons in the modal's header; switching re-scans the library immediately.

Step 2 Multi-Criteria AI Quality Scoring

Every photo in the cluster is evaluated across four objective quality dimensions (0 to 100 points total) to determine the AI Best Shot:

  • Sharpness & Focus (0–40 pts): High-frequency JPEG byte density and face detector focus confidence. Blurry shots receive heavy penalties.
  • Facial Expressions (0–25 pts): Micro-expression analysis awards bonuses for smiling (happy +10 pts) and alert expressions.
  • Eye Openness & Gaze (0–10 pts): Frontal face orientation ensuring subjects look toward camera.
  • Resolution (0–25 pts): Sensor megapixel utilization.
Quality Score = Sharpness (0-40) + Expression Bonus (0-25) + Eye Gaze (0-10) + Resolution (0-25)
Step 3 User Verification & Safe OS Trashing

The Duplicate & Burst Cleaner displays each cluster side-by-side:

  • The AI Best Shot is highlighted with a gold border and labeled with specific reasons (e.g., "Crisp face focus • Smiling expression • Looking at camera").
  • Inferior or blurry duplicates are automatically pre-selected as trash candidates.
  • You Retain Full Control: Click "Keep This Instead" on any frame to change the preferred shot.
  • Zero Permanent Deletion Risk: When you click "Trash Selected Duplicates", files are moved to your Windows Recycle Bin via shell.trashItem so you can restore them anytime if needed.

Biometric Face Recognition & Active Learning

Our face recognition engine operates 100% locally on your computer, running in the background service itself (via ONNX Runtime) — it keeps working even while the app window is closed. Use the search box at the top of the People tab to filter the grid down to a name as you type.

ArcFace 512-D Hypersphere

Each detected face is normalized onto a unit hypersphere ($L_2$ norm). Matches are calculated using Euclidean distance between these normalized embeddings, delivering strong discrimination between different people.

2.5× Active Learning Bonus

When you confirm a face tag or assign a person name, that face receives a 2.5× ground-truth weight multiplier in the person's biometric centroid, immediately finding more photos of them.

Single-Photo Uniqueness

The clustering engine enforces the invariant that one photo cannot contain two identical people. If a merge conflict arises, the app shows the conflict photo for instant 1-click Lightbox resolution.

🔄
Reset & Rescan (Clean Start): Want to start fresh? Click "Reset & Rescan" in the People tab. The app safely deletes all background face records, resets all photo scan flags, and automatically rescans every photo from 1 to Total with a live progress counter.

Zero-Dependency .ZIP Library Backup & Recovery

Export full backups of your photo database, face recognitions, and settings in standard PKZIP format:

Virtual Network Storage & 500px Mirrors

Browse massive network drives (NAS, SMB, external USB) instantly without lag or filling up your local SSD:

  • Lightweight 500px Cache: The app creates lightweight, compressed 500px JPEG thumbnails and JSON EXIF metadata sidecars in C:\GPhotos_VirtualMirrors\[storage_name].
  • Offline Browsing: Browse your entire 500,000+ photo collection smoothly even when disconnected from your local network.
  • Instant Full-Res On-Demand: Clicking a photo immediately fetches the original high-resolution camera file from the network share when online.
  • Direct Folder Tree Explorer: Browse folder subtrees directly even before a full background scan completes.
  • Live Status, Three Numbers: the Network Storage screen shows three always-visible "x / y" rows per storage with a progress bar each — Caching (thumbnails), Face Detection (photos scanned, plus how many faces were actually found), and AI Description (photos captioned by the background auto-tagging feature or a Smart Flow). The left sidebar shows the same three counts compactly as Cache:x/y · Face:x/y · AI:x/y under each storage's name.
  • Rotating a Mirrored Photo: the local thumbnail rotates and updates on screen immediately; the full-resolution original always rotates in the background afterward (reachable or not), so it never sits waiting on a slow network write. If the original can't be rotated after a few attempts, you're notified and the thumbnail is automatically rotated back to match.

Configured Storage Deletion & Unlinked Filter

When you remove a network storage in the Network Mirrors tab:

  • Delete Storage & Free Disk Space: Safely deletes the local 500px mirror cache in C:\GPhotos_VirtualMirrors without ever touching your original network files.
  • Remove from List Only: Keeps thumbnails on disk but unlinks the storage from the app interface.
  • Unlinked Blacklist Guarantee: The storage name is added to a persistent blacklist (gphotos_unlinked_storages_v1) so automatic mirror discovery will never resurrect deleted storages.

Safe In-App Photo Editing & Backup Preservation

Quickly correct orientation or crop photos directly inside the Lightbox:

  • Fit / Fill / 1:1: the buttons next to the lightbox zoom controls choose how each photo opens — Fit (shrunk to the window, the classic view), Fill (enlarged to use as much of the screen as its shape allows) or 1:1 (real pixels, drag to pan). Your choice is remembered.
  • Rotate & Flip: Rotate 90°, 180°, 270°, or flip horizontally.
  • Crop: click "Crop" to drop a handled frame onto the photo, PowerPoint-style — drag inside the frame to move it, or drag any of its 8 corner/edge handles to resize it. Click "Crop" again to hide the frame without losing it, or "Clear Crop" to remove the selection. Only available at 0°/180° rotation.
  • Non-Destructive .bak Protection: When you overwrite an original file, the engine automatically creates a [filename].jpg.bak backup first.
  • Save as Copy: Choose "Save Copy" to export a new [filename]_edited.jpg file while preserving the original unmodified.
  • Requires the Full-Resolution Photo: Edit (and Save Changes/Save Copy) stay disabled, with a tooltip explaining why, until the actual full-resolution photo has finished loading — so a OneDrive/cloud mirror still downloading, or one shown as its cached offline thumbnail, can't get permanently baked in at a lower resolution. HEIC is the one exception, since its editor always works on the local cached preview by design.
  • Thumbnails Update Immediately: saving refreshes the photo's thumbnail right away everywhere it's shown — the gallery grid, albums, People — instead of waiting for a later rebuild.
  • Click a Face Box to Reassign: In the Lightbox, click directly on a face's bounding box to open Assign / Reassign Person for that face immediately — no need to hunt for the small icon on the name label.
  • Auto-Rotate Upright: Select multiple photos in the gallery and choose "Auto-Rotate Upright" — faces are used to detect and correct sideways/upside-down orientation automatically.

Album Chapters

Split a big album (e.g. a wedding, a trip) into named sections — an album with no chapters looks and works exactly as before, nothing changes unless you add one:

  • Create a Chapter: Open an album → "New Chapter" → give it a name (e.g. "Day 1 — Ceremony", "Day 2 — Reception"). Each chapter gets its own heading and its own photo grid inside the album.
  • Auto-Generated Chapter Cover: Every chapter shows a live collage of up to 4 of its own photos next to its title — a visual identity generated on the fly, not a saved file, so it never counts as a photo or shows up in your main gallery.
  • Rename, Reorder, Delete: Click the pencil to rename, or the up/down arrows to reorder chapters. Deleting a chapter never deletes its photos — they simply fall back to the album's "Other Photos" bucket.
  • Adding Photos Asks Which Chapter: The "Add Photos" picker shows a chapter dropdown, defaulting to whichever chapter you used last.
  • Move a Photo Between Chapters: Click the small book icon on any photo tile (once the album has at least one chapter) to reassign it — a photo lives in one chapter at a time, like a folder. You can also just drag a photo onto a chapter; the moment you start dragging, every chapter appears as a target in a bar at the top (plus "Other Photos"), so you never scroll to find the right one.
  • Select Several Photos: like the main gallery — click a photo's small checkmark, or press and drag on empty space (or from a checkmark) to draw a selection box over many photos (Ctrl/Shift adds; the box scrolls the album near the edges). Once photos are selected, clicking a photo toggles it, and dragging any selected photo moves the whole selection to the chapter you drop on. A bar at the bottom offers Move to chapter… (including New chapter with these photos) and works without dragging.
  • Order Photos Inside a Chapter: drop a photo onto another photo of the same chapter to place it just before that one (works across chapters too). Once every photo is in a named chapter, the "Other Photos" section disappears on its own.
  • One-Click Add from the Photo Viewer: The lightbox's quick-add buttons show "Add to Album → Chapter" and go straight there in one click; a small arrow next to it opens a picker if you want a different chapter for that one photo.
  • Adding a Gallery Selection to an Album: select photos and click "Add to Album" — a search-first picker opens (like choosing a person), focused and ready to type, listing every album with its cover photo and name. Type to filter, then Enter (or a click) picks the best match, or creates a new album with that name if nothing matches. Picking (or creating) the album then shows a second step listing that album's chapters — press Enter with nothing typed, or click the pinned "Others" card, to drop the selection into a catch-all "Others" chapter (created the first time, reused after); type a chapter name and Enter to add to it, or type a new name to create one. A back arrow returns to album selection without adding anything.

Smart Flows: Describe a Photo, Automate the Rest

Open Smart Flows from the sidebar to define rules like "a screenshot of a Facebook or LinkedIn post", "a photo of a UPI payment", "a scanned bill" or "a visiting card" — matching photos are automatically added to an album or moved to a folder.

  • Create a Flow: Give it a name, a plain-English description of what to look for, and an action — Add to album (name it) or Move to folder (you pick the destination folder when you run it).
  • Consent Required: Because checking a photo can send it to a cloud AI provider, each flow needs its checkbox ticked once before its first run — there's no default-on path.
  • Run Now, With a Cap: Click "Run now" and choose how many not-yet-checked photos to send this run (default 200) — a flow never silently classifies your whole library. This opens a run window with the flow's name/description, its source, a progress bar, and a live detail log — one line per photo saying exactly what happened ("already described", "trying the local Ollama model...", "sending to the cloud...", matched/not matched, or why it failed). Closing the window doesn't stop the run.
  • Runs Even With No AI Configured: a flow isn't blocked just because no cloud key is set — the shared cache and a local Ollama model (if installed) may already answer some or all photos for free. Whatever genuinely needs the cloud and isn't configured shows a clear error in that photo's log line instead of the run being blocked entirely.
  • Three Free/Cheap Passes Before the Cloud: every photo is checked, in order: (1) a shared local cache — instant, free, remembers photos any flow has ever looked at; (2) a local Ollama vision model, if you have one installed — private and free; only escalating to (3) your configured cloud provider (Gemini/OpenAI, set up under Settings → Smart Flow Configuration — its own, separate settings from AI Search's) when neither of the first two can decide confidently.
  • Smart Flows' Cloud Fallback Is Configured Separately from AI Search: AI Search and Smart Flows are different jobs — searching your existing library vs. auto-sorting new photos — so each gets its own cloud provider and API key, under Settings → AI Search Assistant and Settings → Smart Flow Configuration respectively. Point Search at one provider and Smart Flows at another (or turn either off and rely on the local model/cache alone) — saving one never touches the other's key.
  • Configuring the Local Ollama Engine: install Ollama, then open Settings → Smart Flow Configuration (one section covering both the cloud fallback above and this local model, shared by both AI Search and Smart Flows) — it lists every model Ollama has installed, and if nothing suitable is found yet, shows a "Recommended models to download" list with a one-click Download button (progress bar included, no terminal needed). Pick a vision model and an embedding model from the dropdowns, then "Save & Re-check". A custom host and a Context Window (tokens) field are also available there — see below. If a download fails, the reason is shown under the status line. Model management works from the desktop app only, not from the phone/browser view.
  • Batch Size Adjusts Itself to Your Context Window: Ollama has no API that reports back what context length you've actually set a running model to use, so the Context Window field in Settings is where you tell gPhotos what you configured in Ollama (default 4096, matching Ollama's own default). Before each local run, gPhotos also asks Ollama for that model's own maximum context and uses whichever is smaller, then converts that into a batch size (roughly 360 tokens per photo thumbnail, capped at 20 photos per call). The run log shows the exact numbers at the top of every run. If you've raised Ollama's own context window (e.g. to 32768), set this field to match, or gPhotos will assume the 4096 default and batch more conservatively than your setup allows.
  • Run Against Just One Album — or a Selection: the "Run against" picker in the modal defaults to the whole library but can be pointed at one album instead, so you can try a flow's wording on a small set of photos first. A "Run Smart Flow" button also appears on the selection bar in Photos and inside an Album once you've selected some photos — it opens Smart Flows already scoped to just that selection.
  • AI Info in the Photo Viewer: once a photo has been checked by any flow, its info panel gets an "AI Info" card — an Overview tab with the cached caption/tags, and (once more than one of your flows has checked it) a Smart Flows tab listing each flow's name, match, and confidence.

Create a Video from an Album

Open an album and click "Create Video" to turn its photos into a real MP4 slideshow — rendered with a bundled FFmpeg (nothing to install separately), ready to post to social media. A 6-step wizard:

  • 1. Group Photos: photos are shown under their chapter headings in album order. Select 2-4 and click "Group as Collage" — a popup previews every style (Grid, Side by Side, Stacked, Featured) using your own photos so you can pick one (ungroup again from the same step if needed).
  • 2. Arrange Slides: every slide (photos and collages) listed in order — drag a row (by its handle) or use the up/down arrows to reorder, and uncheck anything to leave it out.
  • 3. Video Settings: aspect ratio (16:9 landscape, 9:16 reels/stories, 1:1 square, 4:3), resolution (720p/1080p), quality/generation speed (draft/good/best), the transition (one effect for every cut, random per cut, or a set of effects you tick and it picks among) and its duration, seconds-per-photo or a target total length, and where to save the .mp4 — pre-filled as <library folder>\videos\<album name>.mp4, changeable with "Change Location".
  • 4. Music (optional): start from the Free music library — 20 royalty-free tracks by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com) with cover art, title, artist, mood and length; filter by mood, Preview a track, then Use it (it downloads on first use, about 8 MB, and is kept). These are Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, so the required credit is added to your end-credits screen automatically. Or choose an audio file from your computer, or paste a YouTube link on the From YouTube tab (uses the free open-source yt-dlp, installed once from the wizard — about 18 MB from its official GitHub release, checksum-verified). Set Start at / End at (m:ss) to choose the part of the song, tick Repeat to fill the whole video to loop it, pick a fade-out and volume, and press Preview this section to listen. Use only music you have the right to use.
  • 5. Title & Credits (optional): design the opening title screen and, on the second tab, closing credits, in the same designer. It previews at your video's exact size and keeps all text inside the frame (wrapping or shrinking it, also on vertical video). Choose a gradient, colour or one of the album's photos as the background (with a darken slider), add / edit / remove text lines with their own font, size, colour, bold/italic, alignment, shadow and position (drag on the preview), and set how long the screen shows.
  • 6. Review & Generate: generate with a live progress bar and a cancel button; the finished video opens automatically in your default player (with "Open Video"/"Show in Folder" also available). A photo that can't be read is skipped and reported, not a failure of the whole video.

AI Photo Search: "Ask AI"

Click "Ask AI" in the Photos toolbar to ask a plain-English question — "Photo of sachin and monika", "Photo of monika alone" — answered by whichever provider is set up under Settings → Search with AI. A match applies as a filter on the gallery.

  • @Mention People, #Mention Places, &Mention Smart Flow Tags: type @ for a dropdown of the people in your library, # for places from your Places map, or & for content tags Smart Flows has already recorded about your photos (e.g. "receipt", "screenshot") — no need to spell any of them exactly, the list matches anywhere in the name (typing "bhavsar" finds both "Sachin Bhavsar" and "Bhavsar Tarun"). Use ↑/↓ and Enter (or click) to pick one, Esc to dismiss the list. Picking an entry drops its plain text into your question, so the sentence still reads naturally. People not yet given a real name aren't offered, since there's nothing to search by yet. A picked place is matched against each photo's actual city and country, even for a combined "City, Country" entry. Tags are stored durably in the library's own database (shared across every Smart Flow, surviving a restart) and only list what a Smart Flow run has actually found — an empty list means run a Smart Flow first; a tagged search only matches photos a Smart Flow has actually analysed.
  • Only these people, nobody else: add the word "only" — "Photo of stavan and stuti only" finds photos with just the two of them, excluding any photo where a third person also appears. "Only photo of monika" (one name) works the same as "photo of monika alone".
  • Excluding someone: add "but not NAME" (also "except NAME" / "without NAME") at the end of the question — "Photo of monika but not raji" finds monika's photos with raji nowhere in them.
  • Combining: "only"/"but not" stack with a place or tag in the same question — "Only photo of monika at #andaman" finds just-monika photos taken in Andaman.

Auto-Describe & Tag Photos (Background)

Settings → Search with AI → "Auto-describe & tag photos in the background" — off by default. When on, gPhotos quietly captions and tags every photo using your local Ollama model (the same one configured for Smart Flows), so &tag search in Ask AI fills in for your whole library over time — no cloud provider is ever used for this, and nothing leaves your machine.

  • Runs in small batches while the app is open and idle; pauses automatically if Ollama isn't reachable and resumes once it is.
  • Backs off while a Smart Flow is running manually, so the two never compete for the same model at once.
  • Resumes exactly where it left off after a restart — an already-captioned photo (by this feature or by any Smart Flow) is never re-sent.
  • The status line under the toggle shows live progress or why it's idle.

Videos in Your Library

Video files (.mp4, .mov, .avi, .mkv, .webm, .wmv, .m4v) in a scanned folder show up in the gallery right alongside your photos — same grid, same albums, same favorites.

  • Each video tile shows a small play-icon badge with its length.
  • Hover over a video tile for about two seconds and a short silent preview clip plays right in the tile; move the mouse away to revert to the still thumbnail. The first hover on a given video generates that preview — every hover after that is instant.
  • Click a video to open it full-screen in gPhotos' own player with normal play/pause/seek controls, the same way a photo opens full-screen.
  • Crop, rotate and the other photo-editing tools simply aren't shown for a video.
  • Videos are not face-scanned, not sent to Smart Flows, and not included in the background auto-tagging pass above — those work on still images only.
  • Got an existing library that predates video support? Click Rescan (Photos toolbar) to re-walk the folder and pick up videos — works for both network/cloud storages and plain local folders. It's safe anytime: it only adds new files and removes deleted ones, never touching favorites/rotation/albums on existing photos. Just reopening the same folder does not do this — once indexed, reopening loads the existing catalog instantly without checking the disk again.

Defensive Error Handling & Crash Recovery

Enterprise-grade stability guarantees to keep your photos and library safe:

  • React Error Boundary: If any component encounters an unexpected rendering error, a recovery screen appears with diagnostic details, a "Try Again" button, and a "Reload Window" button. The app will never show a blank screen.
  • Guarded IPC Handlers: Every native call between the UI and the Windows backend is guarded with try/catch blocks and safe fallback values.
  • Atomic Database Writes: Library state saves use atomic file replacement (.tmp → rename), preventing corruption during unexpected shutdowns.
  • Safe OS Trashing: File deletions are routed to the Windows Recycle Bin so you can restore items anytime.
